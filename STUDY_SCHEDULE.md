# AlignLab — 10-Day Study & Interview-Defence Schedule

**1 September 2026 → 10 September 2026.**

This is a **study schedule only**. Nothing in this file asks you to implement,
retrain, re-run an experiment, or edit a completed phase. The project is
finished; this document tells you how to *learn it* and how to *defend it*.

It is derived from the repository as it actually stands — from
`docs/FINAL_STUDY_HANDOFF.md` (tier ordering, findings F1–F13, study questions
1–28), `docs/EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md` (E1–E23 and the un-numbered runs),
`docs/PROJECT_NARRATIVE.md`, `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`, `HOW_TO_RUN.md` §7 and §9,
and the twenty-five numbered **Checkpoints** that live in `STUDY_WITH_CLAUDE/`.

**Your explain-back sections stay empty.** Every Checkpoint referenced below is
still marked `DEFERRED — USER EXPLAIN-BACK REQUIRED` in the repository, and this
schedule does not answer a single one of them. It only tells you *when* to
attempt each.

**Ordering.** Days 1–4 build the architecture from tensors upward, because every
later phase reads shapes off that model. Days 5–6 take LoRA before SVD/PCA — the
handoff's Tier-1 ordering — so that E17 lands as a challenge to something you
already understand rather than as an abstract spectrum plot. Day 7 is SFT, Day 8
the RLHF material AlignLab studied but did not implement, Day 9 DPO and the
preference metric, Day 10 evaluation and full defence.

## Study Rules

1. **Do not merely read.** A page you have read is not a topic you can defend.
   Reading is the entry cost, not the deliverable.
2. **Derive the important equations by hand** — attention, sinusoidal PE, the
   LoRA parameter count, PCA, the DPO objective. On paper, closed-book, before
   you look at the repository's version.
3. **Reproduce tensor shapes out loud.** Every attention and masking exercise
   ends with you narrating X → Q, K, V → QKᵀ → scores → weights → context →
   merged → output without pausing.
4. **Inspect the actual AlignLab code**, not a mental model of it. The file
   named in the CONCEPTS cell is the file to open — `src/alignlab/…`,
   `scripts/experiments/…`, `configs/…`.
5. **Inspect the actual experiments.** Open the committed JSON in `docs/phase*/`
   and find the number yourself rather than quoting the prose around it.
6. **Perform the explain-backs on the day the schedule names them**, in writing,
   in your own words, in the `STUDY_WITH_CLAUDE/` file where the Checkpoint
   lives.
7. **Do not look at answers before attempting an explanation.** The
   `INTERVIEW_DEFENSE/` model answers are for *checking*, not for learning. Read
   them only once your own attempt exists on the page.
8. **Record uncertainty explicitly.** Write "I cannot yet explain X" next to the
   checkpoint. An honest gap is study material; a vague sense of understanding
   is not.
9. **Revisit failed checkpoints** at the start of the next day, before new
   material. Anything that fails twice goes onto Day 10's mock list.
10. **Preserve the project's terminology.** HOLDS, DISPROVED, NOT CONFIRMED,
    MEASURED, UNTESTABLE AS STATED, POST-HOC, in-distribution, resolvable. These
    words carry the project's honesty; the interview is where they earn it.
11. **Never strengthen a finding.** Where the repository says "consistent with",
    do not say "because". Where it says NOT CONFIRMED, do not say confirmed.
12. **Run only cheap, local, read-only verification** — `python -m pytest -q`,
    or reading a committed JSON artefact. Nothing on this schedule requires a
    GPU, a download, a training run, or any change to the repository.

## Daily Completion Rule

A day is complete when **both** of the following are true:

1. Every concept in that day's **CONCEPTS** cell has been studied against the
   actual repository — the source file opened, the experiment artefact read, and
   the study document worked through; **and**
2. Every item in that day's **CHECKLISTS** cell has been **attempted aloud or in
   writing without relying entirely on notes**, and each attempt has been marked
   **passed**, **shaky**, or **failed**.

A day is **not** complete merely because the reading was finished. It is also
**not** incomplete because a checklist item was failed — a failure that has been
recorded and carried forward *is* a completed attempt.

**Perfect mastery after one day is not required and should not be expected.**
Days 2–4 will need revisiting during Days 5–9, and Day 10 exists precisely
because the first nine days will leave gaps. Carry every **shaky** and
**failed** item into the next morning, and bring the survivors to Day 10's mock.

An explain-back Checkpoint is complete when **you** have written an answer into
the `STUDY_WITH_CLAUDE/` file. This schedule does not answer any of them, and no
day should be marked complete on the strength of an answer you did not write.
